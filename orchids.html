Introduction

Orchids are plants that are popular as houseplants because of their relatively easy care.

There are many different types of orchids, but Phalaenopsis orchids are one of the most common.

Taking care of an orchid mainly means giving it enough light, watering it correctly and keeping it in a suitable environment.

light + water + suitable temperature = healthy orchid

Choosing an orchid

When buying an orchid, you should look at the leaves, roots and flowers before choosing a plant.

A healthy orchid should have firm green leaves and roots that are not soft or rotten.

You should also check the pot to make sure that excess water can drain away.

  • Look for firm leaves.
  • Check that the roots are healthy.
  • Avoid plants with rotten roots.

Light

Light is important because orchids need it to grow and produce flowers.

Most indoor orchids prefer bright, indirect light. Strong direct sunlight can burn their leaves.

If an orchid does not get enough light, it may grow slowly and have trouble producing new flowers.

bright indirect light
  • Keep the plant away from very strong afternoon sunlight.

Watering

Watering is one of the most important parts of orchid care.

Orchids should not stay in water for a long time because constantly wet roots can start to rot.

Instead of following a strict watering schedule, check the roots and growing medium first.

green roots → wait silver roots → water
  • Water when the orchid needs it.
  • Let excess water drain from the pot.
  • Do not leave the pot standing in water.

Temperature and humidity

Orchids generally prefer warm temperatures and moderate humidity.

Normal indoor temperatures are usually suitable.

Try to keep the plant away from cold drafts and heating devices such as radiators.

18°C - 27°C
  • Avoid sudden temperature changes.
  • Keep the orchid away from radiators.
  • Good air circulation is useful.

Repotting

Orchids do not need to be repotted very often.

Normal garden soil should not be used because orchid roots need air around them.

Special orchid mixes usually contain materials such as bark that allow water to drain and air to reach the roots.

orchid roots need air
  • Use a pot with drainage holes.
  • Use an orchid growing mix.
  • Remove rotten roots when repotting.

Common problems

Many orchid problems are caused by incorrect watering or unsuitable light.

Yellow leaves do not always mean that the orchid is dying. An old leaf can naturally turn yellow and fall off.

Soft or dark roots can be a sign of root rot, especially when the plant has been kept too wet.

too much water → root problems
  • Check the roots if the plant looks unhealthy.
  • Move the plant if its leaves are getting burned.
  • Do not water again if the growing medium is still wet.
